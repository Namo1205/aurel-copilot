What Aurel should build with AI

Strategy memo · To: COO · From: AI Product Manager · 23 October 2026
Terms. The reservations desk is the 22-person team that answers every guest contact before arrival (the brief calls it the CRO). The PMS is the hotel's booking system. A trace is a to-do in the PMS sent to a hotel department with a due date. AI agent is the software; agent on its own is a person on the desk.

2Who we are solving for, and why

3Vision: two to three years

4The one bet for these two quarters: the Aurel Reservations AI Agent

Sort every message by type, then send each type to the cheapest safe handler. Documents for policy questions. The PMS for prices. People for judgement calls. Traces for the hotels.

5What we will not build

A bot that books and takes payment aloneThe PMS cannot take a card in a message. We need Q1 approval data before anything books unsupervised.
Phone or voiceLive answers in seconds with no approve step, recording consent, no transcripts today, and no engineer capacity. Year 2, as assist first.
Discounting or price matchingPrices are the same everywhere by agreement with the OTAs, and the PMS cannot discount. Nothing to negotiate.
Group pricingThe groups specialists own it. The AI agent only passes groups on with the details.
A website chatbotA new channel. Not where the problem is. Year 2 once the AI agent is trusted.

6Where AI is the wrong answer

7Reply to the COO

An AI agent taking bookings on every channel in 3 months
Yes to the AI agent. Email in Q1, WhatsApp by end Q1, OTA messaging in Q2, phone in year 2. Not because code is slow, but because channel access, GDPR sign-off and proof of safety take real weeks, and two engineers can only build so much. Policy questions are answered alone from the first live week, after two weeks of shadow mode. Everything else is approved by a person until the AI agent earns it. The last bot was switched off for one bad promise; this one never confirms what the hotel has not.
24/7
By end of Q1, within the hour in opening hours. By Q2, around the clock for the types that send alone. Year 2 for the rest.
Headcount down 40% by year end
The numbers don't support it. Thirteen people would be at least 99% busy even if the AI agent delivers everything I estimate, which is today's problem again. I am not planning a reduction. Nine people leave every year anyway; whether we replace them is decided on measured hours at the end of Q1 and again at the end of Q2, not now. Headcount is worth about EUR 0.4M a year at most (9 people × EUR 45k). The conversion gap, 34% versus 9%, is worth EUR 5M to 9M. That is the prize.
Tell me the plan
This memo, the PRD with the AI agent's behaviour spec and metrics, and a working prototype. From you: the three work changes in section 4; a named sponsor at each hotel for traces, with the "promises kept" number in your weekly review; and a message to the team in week one: the target is speed and kept promises, and nobody is being replaced by the tool they are training. Inside the desk the tool is called Aurel Assistant, never "the AI", for the same reason.

Assumptions written down. 22 people × 37.5 productive hours = 825 h a week; 2 managers, 3 groups and events specialists, 4 seniors, 13 agents; one shared queue; desk open 07:00–23:00, seven days. Average booking EUR 1,350 (three nights × EUR 450); loaded cost per agent EUR 45k. Handle-time reductions behind the impact figures are estimates to be measured in shadow mode. Email and WhatsApp APIs available; OTA partner APIs need approval. PMS traces accept a department, due date and restricted flag; if not, sensitive detail stays in our system with a pointer. Two engineers for two quarters (~52 engineer-weeks) cover PMS integration and traces, email, WhatsApp, the AI agent, the desk, guardrails and a pilot, with a five-week buffer.
